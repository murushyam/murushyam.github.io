Chapter 01 · Origin & Mission

Coupling fire weather, carbon pools, & satellite time series.

Trained in geoinformatics and atmospheric science, I view energy budgets, soil moisture memory, and canopy structure as one interconnected planetary system.

Forest fires across South Asia are accelerating in frequency and severity. My doctoral thesis, "Decoding Atmosphere–Biosphere Flux Nexus in the Phase of Wildfire using AI Techniques over Heterogeneous Forest Ecosystems", investigates three contrasting ecoclimatic regimes: moist deciduous Similipal, dry deciduous Bandhavgarh, and montane subtropical forests of Chamba / Uttarakhand through pre-fire, active fire, and multi-year recovery trajectories.

By fusing microwave and optical remote sensing (MODIS, Sentinel-2, AVIRIS-NG, InSAR) with high-resolution land surface flux diagnostics (sensible and latent heat fluxes, vapor pressure deficit, soil moisture, and aerosol optical depth), I develop hybrid prognostic frameworks spanning WRF-Chem atmospheric simulations to attention-augmented deep learning, causal fuzzy time series, and parameterized quantum circuits.

Beyond the thesis, my research extends to explainable AI for urban PM2.5 nowcasting in Singapore, atmospheric methane perturbations during the COVID-19 lockdown, hyperspectral inland water quality retrieval over Vembanad Lake, ground subsidence mapping with radar interferometry, and quantum algorithms through the global Womanium Quantum + AI Fellowship.

Garhwal Himalaya · Montane

Chamba & Uttarakhand Forests

High-altitude conifer buffer: SH jumped +42.9% while LH had a milder -9.9% decline. The 2022 fires showed rapid monsoon recharge with 40.85% post-fire recovery and +14.35% net gain.

SH +42.9% LH -9.9% Recovery 40.85% Net +14.35%

Similipal Biosphere Reserve

Mayurbhanj, Odisha · [21.86° N, 86.34° E]

During the 25–29 April 2021 fire, sensible heat flux surged by +22.4% while latent heat collapsed by -33%. Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) reached an extreme 5.04 ± 0.41 kPa (+94.6% spike), elevating LST by ~5 °C. Over 47% of above-ground biomass was lost. Strong soil-moisture feedback acted as the primary driver turning burn scars into persistent hyper-arid heating zones.
